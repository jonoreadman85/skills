# Reading CV evidence reliably

## PDF and Word layout

Account for every supplied page or document section, including tables, before calling a source fully reviewed. With a PDF, preserve the original page numbers. For multi-column pages or suspicious extraction order, inspect the rendered page and verify column boundaries before connecting a claim to a heading, employer, dates or project. Never infer those relationships from extraction order alone.

For Word, read tables as well as paragraphs. Where text boxes, images or missing content could affect coverage, inspect a rendered document or another reader that exposes those objects. Use rendered page citations only when pagination was verified. Otherwise cite section headings and a short identifying excerpt; do not invent Word page numbers. Plain text can use supplied headings or stable line references.

Use available local readers and rendering tools. Reading text is enough for a straightforward text source; do not render every document mechanically. Keep originals unchanged and do not send CVs to an external extraction service merely to complete a review.

## Extraction failures and coverage

For blank, garbled or incomplete extraction, try rendering or local OCR when available. Do not turn uncertain OCR into a factual claim. Record uncertain text and the affected location; request a readable source only if needed for a material conclusion. Continue other readable inputs.

Use **partially reviewed** when some content was assessed and some remains unreadable or unresolved. Use **blocked** when no substantive review is possible, with the access, identity or extraction reason. These are source states, not competency ratings. A page that is blank in extraction has not been shown to be blank in the original.

Scope every negative conclusion to actual coverage: “No testing evidence found on readable pages 1–2; page 3 could not be reviewed.” Do not say the whole CV lacks testing or classify inaccessible material as a candidate gap. When full coverage cannot be established, retain that limitation in the summary and recommendation.
