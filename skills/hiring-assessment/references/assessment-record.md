Use this evidence relationship for each material role requirement. Combine related items and adapt the layout rather than forcing every CV into an identical length. All content below is synthetic.

## Requirement and evidence

**Requirement:** independently coordinate service incidents and communicate recovery plans.

**Source evidence:** CV, Experience / ExampleCo, second bullet: describes coordinating three teams during service disruption and introducing customer updates. Label: described ownership; unverified CV claim.

**Interpretation:** relevant incident-coordination experience. The CV does not establish how decisions were made or whether recovery improved. Missing measurements remain unknown.

**Interview probe:** walk through one incident, your own decisions, the trade-offs, communications and evidence that recovery was sustained. What would you change?

**Recruiter reconciliation:** overview says there is no incident experience. That conflicts with the cited CV example; investigate depth rather than repeat the overview's conclusion.

## Separate activities, attribution and uncertainty

Synthetic source S002, Experience / ExampleCo: “Implemented queue consumers; the team reduced failures by 40%. Helped migrate billing APIs.”

- “Implemented queue consumers”: **described delivery**. The activity is specific even though design ownership, release responsibility and outcome are unknown.
- “The team reduced failures by 40%”: a separately attributed, unverified **team outcome**. Ask about baseline, measurement and the person's contribution; do not label it personal ownership.
- “Helped migrate billing APIs”: **unclear** personal activity. Ask which task or decision was theirs. This differs from no migration statement being present.
- If a confirmed second version says 15% instead of 40%, preserve both source references and ask whether periods or measures differ. Do not choose one figure or infer dishonesty.

If another page cannot be read, absence statements apply only to the reviewed sections. Source coverage is recorded separately from the evidence labels above.

## Worked screening decisions

These synthetic examples illustrate a user who requests **Yes / No + reason / Second opinion**. They are screening recommendations for human review, not final hiring decisions. Use another user's chosen labels and format when those differ. Retain source references and reasoning in the assessment record; a requested names-and-outcomes reply can contain just those two fields.

### Yes: validate depth during the agreed assessment

**Brief:** Senior software engineer; production cloud delivery and practical LLM experience. The agreed next stage is the technical assessment.

**Evidence:** Candidate B, CV page 2 / ExampleCo, describes deploying a tool-using support agent and operating its API. Production deployment is described; scale, personal ownership and failure recovery remain unverified.

**Decision:** **Yes — validate production experience and personal ownership during the technical assessment.**

The CV provides enough relevant evidence to progress. Carry the unresolved questions into the agreed assessment; do not introduce an additional preliminary screen merely because CV claims need validation. If a missing fact materially determines eligibility or progression, identify it explicitly rather than hiding it in a routine interview probe.

### Second opinion: resolve a specific level question

**Brief:** Senior engineer, including independent technical direction and influence across teams.

**Evidence:** Candidate C, CV page 1 / Project Atlas, describes implementing a relevant production service. The phrase “led the platform” does not establish decision authority or cross-team scope. Delivery is described; Senior ownership is unclear.

**Decision:** **Second opinion — the Senior hiring manager or designated technical reviewer should decide whether the cited project evidence supports the advertised Senior scope.** Ask the reviewer to distinguish implementation responsibility from independent architecture and cross-team influence. If the CV cannot resolve that distinction, the reviewer should decide whether to progress and validate it in the existing assessment or seek a targeted clarification.

This identifies the uncertainty, reviewer role and decision. It does not create a new interview stage, assume a Mid vacancy exists or send the referral automatically. A generic “needs more validation” is not a useful second-opinion request.

### Experience during study: apply the actual requirement

**Brief:** Three years of relevant software engineering; no rule excludes work undertaken during study.

**Evidence:** Candidate D, CV page 1 / Experience, describes a software-development role overlapping a degree. Employment dates are supplied, but working hours are not.

**Interpretation:** Assess the described engineering work on its evidence. Do not subtract it solely because it overlaps study, or invent a post-graduation-only requirement. Avoid double-counting overlapping calendar periods. If duration or workload ambiguity changes whether the stated threshold is met, identify the affected period and ask recruiting or the hiring manager to resolve how it satisfies the requirement; otherwise retain it as a clarification without changing the outcome.

**Decision where the threshold is otherwise supported:** **Yes — validate the relevant delivery claims during the agreed assessment.** If the threshold remains material and unresolved, state that uncertainty and route it to the appropriate human determination rather than declaring the work ineligible.

### No: give the role-related reason

**Brief:** A vacancy requires production LLM-system delivery.

**Evidence:** Candidate E, CV pages 1–2, describes general software delivery and lists use of a coding assistant. No LLM application or agent-system delivery is evidenced in the fully reviewed CV.

**Decision:** **No — the reviewed CV does not evidence the production LLM-system delivery required for this vacancy.**

This explains the evidence gap without asserting inability or treating coding-assistant use as building an LLM system. If relevant pages were unreadable, retain that coverage limitation and defer a conclusion that depends on them.

### Later revision: keep saved reports interpretable

**Earlier record:** Candidate F was marked Second opinion because Senior ownership was unclear.

**Later instruction:** After reviewing additional project evidence, the hiring manager changes the outcome to Yes and requests an updated reply draft.

**Handling:** Use Yes in the reply draft and update the saved assessment already within the task scope. Record: “Current outcome: Yes; supersedes the earlier Second opinion recommendation following the hiring manager's review of the additional project evidence.” Retain the prior rationale and reference the new evidence. Update any affected overview generated for the same task, or flag a known inaccessible copy as outdated. Do not silently overwrite independent interviewer records, alter unrelated historical packs or claim an external email/recruiting record was updated.

Changing “progress to technical assessment” to the user's label “Yes” alone does not establish a substantive reversal. Align the wording without inventing new evidence or a changed judgement.

## When information is missing

- Without a target role or level, produce a sourced evidence summary and ask for the missing role/level; defer suitability and grade recommendations. Do not infer a Senior role from the current candidate folder or from tenure.
- Without repository context, assess the supplied requirements directly. Do not request a repository for a role that does not need one.
- Without a relevant playbook, identify the missing interview guidance or propose a clearly labelled structure; do not silently use the AI engineering pilot.
- Without interview observations, retain CV evidence labels and validation questions. Do not award interview-performance ratings from the CV.
